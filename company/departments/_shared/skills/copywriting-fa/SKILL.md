# SpielOS Persian Copywriting System

## Mission

Turn product work, founder sessions, technical decisions, launches, failures, experiments, and internal discussions into useful Persian content for the canonical ICP.

This is not a translation skill. It creates original Persian content. The source material is evidence for the story, not the story structure.

A work session is evidence, not the article. The reader's problem determines the story.

## Reference files

Before writing, read:

- `../../../company/strategy/icp.md` — the single source of truth for the customer profile, exclusions, and positioning idea. Never restate the ICP in this file; read it from the source.
- `../../../company/strategy/voice.md` — the company voice and canonical One Idea Hierarchy.
- `../translation-fa/persian-glossary.md` — the single source of truth for all terminology
- `../translation-fa/SKILL.md` — for sentence-level Persian quality rules, voice, register, and validation

The ICP is identical in English and Persian. The behavioral profile does not change with language. Only the linguistic and cultural expression changes.

---

## ICP summary (operational)

The primary buyer is the owner, CEO, COO, or senior operator of an established online business or service provider ($1M–$25M+ revenue) with real customers, a working funnel, and high-volume repetitive knowledge-work done by hand — intake, delivery, support, reporting, scheduling, triage.

They are not technical. They do not build harnesses and they do not care about agent architecture. They care about labor cost, throughput, delivery speed, and errors in the repetitive work their team does manually.

They are skeptical of generic AI claims. They want to see real work, real tradeoffs, and real constraints.

In Persian: صاحب یا مدیر یک کسب‌وکار آنلاین مستقر با مشتری واقعیه که کارهای تکراری زیادی رو هنوز دستی انجام می‌ده. فنی نیست؛ به معماری ایجنت اهمیت نمی‌ده. به هزینه نیرو، سرعت تحویل و خطاهای کار دستی اهمیت می‌ده.

## Required inputs

Before writing, determine:

1. **Surface**: note, article, landing page, feature page, founder story, X post, LinkedIn post, email, documentation, or changelog
2. **ICP segment**: Which awareness level? (problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware)
3. **Main problem**: What is the reader struggling with right now?
4. **Desired outcome**: What should the reader understand, feel, or do after reading?
5. **Evidence from the work session**: What concrete examples support the argument?
6. **Product truth**: What does SpielOS actually do? What is it not?
7. **Search intent**: When relevant, what would the reader type into a search engine?
8. **CTA**: What should the reader do next?
9. **Tone and length**: How long? How formal? How technical?
10. **Language**: Persian-first, not English-copy translated later

If these are inferable from the source material, do not ask unnecessary questions.

---

## ICP simulation (Persian)

Before drafting, simulate the intended reader. For each piece, internally answer:

1. What problem is this reader already experiencing?
2. How would an Iranian founder describe this problem aloud?
3. Which English technical terms are already natural for them? (AI, model, prompt, agent, API — these keep English)
4. Which terms require explanation? (harness, orchestration, evals, context — explain before naming)
5. Which translated search phrases sound artificial? (avoid forced calques)
6. What do they misunderstand about AI employees?
7. What would make them stop reading?
8. What proof would they trust?
9. What practical lesson can they apply?
10. What question should the article answer by the end?
11. Whether the sentence works without its English source

Write for that reader, not for the person who attended the internal session.

Do not expose this simulation in the final article unless requested.

## Awareness levels

### Problem-aware (آگاه از مشکل)

The reader knows their AI workflows are fragmented or unreliable but may not know what an agent harness is.

Lead with:

- تکرار دستورات در هر جلسه
- از دست رفتن کانتکست
- ابزارهای پراکنده
- خروجی ناسازگار
- بنیان‌گذار به‌عنوان گلوگاه
- ایجنت‌هایی که فقط چت می‌کنن و نمی‌تونن اجرا کنن

Do not assume they know what roles, skills, workflows, or evals are.

### Solution-aware (آگاه از راه‌حل)

The reader understands agents, automations, or AI employees but is comparing approaches.

Explain:

- نقش‌ها و دستورات
- مهارت‌ها
- ورک‌فلوها
- حافظه و کانتکست
- اتصال ابزارها
- تأیید انسانی
- ارزیابی
- اجرای Direct و Director

### Product-aware (آگاه از SpielOS)

The reader already knows SpielOS.

Show:

- How the product works
- Specific capabilities
- Real examples
- Tradeoffs
- Setup requirements
- What is currently available

Do not write every page as if it addresses all three levels.

## Buying triggers (محرک‌های خرید)

Typical triggers include:

- بنیان‌گذار وقت زیادی رو صرف کارهای تکراری عملیاتی می‌کنه
- تیم پرامپت‌ها و اتوماسیون‌های پراکنده جمع کرده
- یه ایجنت پروتوتایپ کار کرده ولی نتونسته تبدیل به یه ورک‌فلوی قابل اعتماد بشه
- شرکت می‌خواد AI چند بخش رو هماهنگ انجام بده
- ابزارهای موجود فقط چت یا اتوماسیون ساده دارن
- بنیان‌گذار می‌خوade خروجی رو بدون استخدام نیروی جدید بیشتر کنه
- تیم به تأیید، ردیابی، کانتکست و کنترل کیفیت نیاز داره

## Primary objections (اعتراضات اصلی)

Copy should address objections when relevant:

- آیا این یه چت‌بات یا رپ‌لایزر دیگه‌ست؟
- چقدر راه‌اندازی نیاز داره؟
- می‌تونم کنترل کنم ایجنت‌ها چی کار کنن؟
- آیا آدم‌ها می‌تونن کارهای مهم رو تأیید کنن؟
- با مدل‌ها و ابزارهای من کار می‌کنه؟
- کانتکست شرکت رو یادش می‌مونه؟
- می‌تونه ورک‌فلوی واقعی اجرا کنه یا فقط جواب می‌ده؟
- چطور مطمئن بشم خروجیش قابل اعتماده؟
- یه تیم کوچیک بدون زیرساخت سنگین می‌تونه ازش استفاده کنه؟

Do not answer objections with unsupported superlatives.

## Session-to-content transformation

A work session is evidence, not the article structure.

From the session, extract:

- **Trigger**: What started the work?
- **Problem**: What was broken, unclear, or risky?
- **Tension**: What initially looked correct but was not?
- **Decision**: What changed?
- **Evidence**: What concrete examples support the decision?
- **Outcome**: What became clearer, faster, safer, or more maintainable?
- **Lesson**: What can the ICP learn?
- **Limitation**: Where does the lesson not apply universally?
- **Action**: What should the reader do next?

Then design the content around the reader's problem.

Never write chronologically:

> اول واژه‌نامه رو عوض کردیم. بعد مهارت رو ویرایش کردیم. بعد مقاله رو تست کردیم.

Write around the reader's problem:

> ترجمه‌ی بد همیشه از مدل بد نمیاد. گاهی محصول هنوز تصمیم نگرفته با چه زبانی خودش رو توضیح بده. این مشکل وقتی مشخص شد که سه صفحه‌ی SpielOS برای یک مفهوم، سه ترجمه‌ی متفاوت داشتن.

The session provides examples. The reader's problem determines the story.

## Long-form article structure

For SpielOS building notes, default to:

1. **Contextual opening** — a concrete observation, mistake, or decision
2. **Concrete problem** — what was broken, unclear, or risky
3. **Example or evidence** — a real case from the work
4. **Why the obvious explanation was incomplete** — the tension
5. **Decision or change** — what was done differently
6. **Result** — what became better
7. **Nuance or limitation** — where this lesson does not apply
8. **Practical lesson for the ICP** — what the reader can use
9. **Relevant CTA** — what to do next

Do not force this structure when another format is more appropriate, but every article must contain a clear progression from problem to resolution.

## Openings

An opening must not start with an abstract thesis unless the reader already has the required context.

Prefer:

- A concrete observation
- A mistake
- A surprising output
- A conflict
- A before/after contrast
- A specific user problem
- A decision with consequences

Bad:

> همه یک تاکسونومی بازاریابی می‌خوان.

This assumes the reader already knows why taxonomy matters. They do not.

Better:

> وقتی از یک ایجنت خواستم ساختار سئوی SpielOS رو طراحی کنه، برای محصول ده‌ها صفحه‌ی تیم، راه‌حل و تمپلیت ساخت؛ صفحاتی برای قابلیت‌هایی که هنوز در محصول وجود نداشتن.

Now the reader has a concrete situation to react to.

## One Idea Hierarchy

Before drafting, lock three levels:

1. **Company promise connection** — identify how the topic relates to more
   output, lower operating cost, or improving one workflow at a time.
2. **Topic-specific idea** — choose one problem, mechanism, objection, lesson,
   proof point, or outcome for this Persian asset. Different articles and
   formats may explain different topics.
3. **Asset execution** — write one natural Persian title and one coherent
   argument. Every example, section, visual cue, and CTA serves that topic.

If the reader remembers one idea, decide what it should be and why an Iranian
founder would care. State it clearly, then expand. Do not force a literal
translation of the English title, and do not repeat the company promise as
every headline.

## Titles

Titles must communicate one clear tension, problem, result, or lesson.

Avoid vague philosophical titles that sound intelligent but overstate the argument.

Weak:

> سئو نتیجه‌ی جانبی معماریه

Stronger:

> نذار SEO یک محصول خیالی برای سایتت بسازه

Or:

> چرا بیشتر صفحه‌های پیشنهادی AI رو از سایت حذف کردم

Titles should be understandable without reading the article. If the title requires the article to make sense, rewrite the title.

---

## Evidence over slogans

Use the work session to provide:

- Real examples
- Before/after copy
- Decisions
- Removed pages
- Failed translations
- Changed architecture
- Product constraints
- Tradeoffs

Limit slogan-like sentences. One strong closing line is useful. Six dramatic contrasts in one article make the writing sound generated.

Reject repeated constructions such as:

- X compounds; Y does not.
- One architecture, two languages, zero fiction.
- Stop optimizing X. Optimize Y.
- X is not a description. It is the product.
- Everything else is decoration.
- وسوسه‌ی بزرگ یه داستان موازیه.

Keep a line only when it adds meaning rather than rhythm.

---

## Product truth and customer language

Do not confuse product architecture with marketing architecture.

**Product architecture** is the source of truth for what SpielOS actually does.

**Customer language** determines how those capabilities should be explained.

**Search research** determines how people look for those capabilities.

A valid page must connect a real user need to a capability SpielOS genuinely supports.

Do not invent:

- Fake features
- Fake use cases
- Fake teams
- Fake templates
- Fake results
- Fake customer stories
- Fake search demand

But do not limit the site only to internal product navigation labels. Customers do not search for "harness" or "orchestration" — they search for "how to make AI work as a team" or "AI employee setup."

In Persian: مشتری‌ها دنبال «هارنس» یا «ارکستریشن» نمی‌گردن — دنبال «ایجنت AI که کار تکراری رو انجام بده» یا «تیم AI برای شرکت» می‌گردن.

## Persian SEO localization

Do not translate English keywords directly and call it Persian SEO.

For Persian content:

- Identify how Iranian users describe the problem
- Prefer phrases people naturally type or say
- Distinguish technical builders from general founders
- Write titles and headings for Persian comprehension first
- Do not force awkward translated keywords into every paragraph
- Treat English and Persian as two markets sharing product truth, not two copies sharing sentences

Shared routes and components simplify implementation. They do not replace Persian keyword research or Persian copywriting.

## Titles and headings in Persian

Persian titles should:

- Use natural word order for Persian, not English word order
- Avoid calques that only make sense when reverse-translated
- Communicate the point without requiring the article
- Use the glossary terms correctly

Bad:

> معماری جواب است

This is a philosophical fragment that requires the article to decode.

Good:

> چرا ساختار سایتت باید با محصولت هماهنگ باشه

---

## Persian sentence completeness in copywriting

The same sentence completeness rules from the translation skill apply here. Every sentence must be a complete, useful thought.

Bad:

> وسوسه‌ی بزرگ یه داستان موازیه.

Good:

> خطر اینجاست که سایت شروع کنه نسخه‌ای خیالی از محصول رو توصیف کنه.

Bad:

> جستجو همون چیزیه که محصوله.

Good:

> صفحه‌های سایت باید دقیقاً همون چیزی رو توضیح بدن که محصول واقعاً انجام می‌ده — نه یه نسخه‌ی خیالی ازش.

---

## Natural Persian collocations in copywriting

The same collocation rules from the translation skill apply here. Do not use combinations that only make sense in English.

Reject:

- حقیقت محصول بهره‌ی مرکب می‌ده
- برق بازاریابی
- داستان موازی
- معماری جواب است
- صفحه جای خودش را به دست می‌آورد
- ساختار آینه‌ی هارنس است

Rewrite using Persian expressions that naturally occur together.

## Persian-specific simulation questions

Before drafting any Persian piece, also answer:

1. How would an Iranian founder describe this problem aloud?
2. Which English terms are already natural in their speech? (AI, model, prompt, agent, API — keep English)
3. Which terms need explanation before being named? (harness → سیستم مدیریت ایجنت, orchestration → هماهنگ‌سازی چند ایجنت)
4. Which translated search phrases sound artificial? (avoid forced calques)
5. Does each sentence work without its English source?
6. Is the register appropriate for an educated Iranian founder?

---

## Quality gates

Reject the content if any answer is no:

- Does the reader know what is happening from the opening?
- Is the ICP identifiable?
- Does the piece solve or clarify a real problem?
- Is the session used as evidence rather than chronology?
- Are the examples concrete?
- Does the argument include necessary nuance?
- Is the Persian natural without the English source?
- Does every sentence have a clear purpose?
- Are technical terms explained or justified?
- Is the article specific to SpielOS?
- Could this copy belong to any generic AI startup?
- Does the CTA naturally follow from the article?
- Has unsupported marketing language been removed?
- Does the asset communicate one topic-specific idea connected naturally to the company promise?
- Do the Persian title, argument, evidence, and CTA all serve that same idea?

## Shared bilingual content rule

English and Persian versions may share:

- Product truth
- Facts
- Evidence
- Core argument
- Product capabilities
- High-level page purpose

They must not be forced to share:

- Sentence structure
- Paragraph boundaries
- Metaphors
- Headlines
- Search phrases
- Examples
- Transitions
- CTA wording
- Level of explicit context

The Persian article is not the English article with substituted vocabulary.

The English article is not the Persian article written first and translated backward.

Both are original expressions of the same supported message for the same behavioral ICP.

---

## Output rules

Return only the final Persian copy unless notes or alternatives are requested.

Preserve:

- Product names (SpielOS, اشپیل)
- Code references, file paths, model names
- URLs, IDs, placeholders
- Markdown structure when required

When the source material is insufficient for a claim, mark the gap instead of inventing evidence.
